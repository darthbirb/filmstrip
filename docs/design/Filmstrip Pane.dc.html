The pane, in each state it has
Four parts, always in this order: a header row that is a disclosure, the picture on the panel, an action bar, and a filmstrip along the foot. There is one mode — no preview mode and grid mode, and no back arrow, because there is no history for it to carry. Each drawing below is 20rem wide and 34rem tall, the pane's own width at 100%. The bar is absent from the two states that have no file to act on; the full-screen control is absent from those two and from the rail, which has no header row to hold it.
What the pane knows is read from the file when its thumbnail is made: a capture date only if the file's own metadata carries one, a video's length and codec from ffprobe, and a rotated recording's true shape. Nothing is guessed, and every row is named for what it actually is.

Why the header is the disclosure
A pane 20rem wide cannot afford a headline, and the file's name is the least useful thing at a glance: you clicked the picture, you know which one it is. The shape and the size are what you actually check, so they take the row you always see — and they double as the handle for everything else. The name is the last of the details, not a title. A heading only a screen reader reads keeps the pane findable.
Why the strip is a fixed shape
Every frame is 4:3 whatever the picture is, so the strip is a ruler you can count along rather than a second grid with its own rhythm. It runs through the place the item was clicked in, not what the grid shows now — the pane keeps what it holds while you look somewhere else, and its strip keeps with it. Like the grid, only the frames near the scroll are drawn.
What is not here
No back arrow and no mode switch. ggallery's drawing has both, and each waits on something that does not exist: history, and sets. Its zoom readout was the third, and it is drawn now — at the foot of this sheet, because the picture can zoom. The action bar and the favourite are the two that have since been built, which is why they are drawn below and in every state above. Nothing is drawn with nothing behind it — a control that cannot act is worse than an absence, because it has to be explained.

A picture that only fills a third of it turns the other two thirds into furniture, and the eye reads the box rather than the photograph. The plate is as wide as the picture it sits on rather than as wide as the pane. The landscape clip drawn above gets 288px of it and keeps the frame steps, dropping only the speed; a portrait clip in that same 20rem pane gets about 190px, which is the 210px plate here. Below that the total goes and the elapsed stays, at 165px here — not an abbreviation and not a rule of its own: the total is the same figure the details row above already reads, 1920 × 1080 · 0:12 · 2.3 MB, so the plate may lose it without the window losing it. The elapsed is on screen nowhere else, so it is the one figure that never leaves — and the slash leaves with the total rather than trailing a number with nothing after it. The mute is last out, at 120px.
Play and pause are one controlIt is the only glyph in the plate drawn filled and in in-pane white, because it is the one the pointer goes for; everything beside it is fg-mid and comes to full ink when it is doing something — muted, or off 1×. The track is 0.1875rem, the same as the indexing line, both are a proportion. It does seek. The line is 0.1875rem but its target is the plate's whole 2rem height across the track's width, so a 3px rule has a 32px hit area; under the pointer it thickens to 0.375rem and a 0.625rem thumb appears at the position. Dragging moves the picture with it and the elapsed figure reads the drag's target, not where playback was. The plate stays up while the pointer is inside it and for 1.5s after it leaves.
The bar fills from the left and the ⋯ holds the remainderThe same measured rule the grid's header uses: nothing is dropped at a width someone wrote down. Favourite and Move to… are the two that always show, because they are the two that change the library. Each glyph button that does not fit moves into the ⋯ menu in the order written, and the menu is always there — so the list of what the app can do to a file never depends on the window.
The controls are over the picture, not under itA second 2.75rem row under a picture in a 34rem pane would take the picture's last quarter and leave the filmstrip nothing. On the glass they cost the picture's bottom 2rem, which is where a video's controls have always been, and they are the app's one dark glass — the same plate a tile's length wears.
Favourite is not yellowggallery's star was #e3bf46 and that is an accent this app does not have. The state is carried by the same thing every other toggle carries it with: the filled glyph and the raised-hi plate. A flag that needs a hue to be legible is a flag drawn too small.

A picker on the button, not a window over the appChoosing a folder is the same act as choosing one in navigation, so it is the same list: the tree, at menu width, with the one shadow the app has. A dialog would scrim the pictures to ask a question the panel beside it already answers, and Settings is deliberately the only thing that sits over the window. The filter is there because a hundred-folder tree in a 16rem menu is a scroll, and the three you actually use are at the top under Recent.
A clash is reported, never resolved for youThe move is per file and it never overwrites: what moved stays moved, what could not is left exactly where it was, and the count in the header says both. A part-way failure across a selection is therefore not a special case — it is the same list with more rows. It reads over the grid in the failures banner's own shape, because it is the same kind of fact: something only you can settle, staying in the way until dismissed. Renaming the clash and moving it again is the fix, which is why rename is drawn here too.
Rename is an edit of the row that holds the nameThe details already show the name, last, as the least of the facts about a file. A dialog to change one field that is on screen is furniture, so the ⋯ item opens the details if they are shut and puts the cursor in that row — which also means the rename is drawn where its result will be. The extension is shown and editable but never selected: it can be changed on purpose and not by typing.
zoomThe wheel zooms the picture, and dragging pans itported from ggallery · ×1.12 a notch · 0.1× to 12×
The behaviour is settled and comes over as it stands: the wheel zooms about the point under the pointer, starting from the scale the fitted picture is already drawn at, so the first notch out of fit does not jump. A notch is ×1.12 out and ×0.89 back, held between 0.1× and 12×. Dragging pans. Double-click returns to fit, and so does showing another item. What had to be drawn is the furniture — and the answer is that at fit there is none at all, and off fit there is exactly one thing: a percentage, which is also the way back.

The three specimens are live: the pointer says a picture can be dragged only once there is somewhere to drag it to, and closes while it is held. At fit it is the ordinary arrow, because at fit a drag does nothing.
The keyboard zooms about the centre, because it has nowhere else toWhile the focus is on the picture — which the red ring says — + and − take the same ×1.12 notch about the middle of the view, 0 returns to fit, and the arrows pan a tenth of the view at a time once there is anywhere to pan. In the grid the arrows still step tiles: the keys answer to whichever of the two has the ring, and nothing is bound twice.
A video does not zoomA clip's bottom 2rem is its controls, and its track is the one thing in the app a drag across a picture already means. Zooming would put a plate over a plate, and panning would fight the seek. So the wheel over a video does nothing and the pointer stays the arrow — the same answer as a codec the window cannot play, where there is a poster and no player. If a zoomed clip is ever wanted it is the controls that have to move first, which is a change to the plate and not to this.
Resizing keeps the zoom; the next picture does notWhat is held is a multiple of fit and the point at the centre of the view, never a pixel size. So dragging the splitter, opening the details, folding the pane and going full screen all re-fit and keep both — the picture you were looking into is still the picture you are looking into, at the same magnification. Showing another item returns to fit, because a zoom is about one photograph and carrying it to the next would land a person somewhere in the middle of a picture they have not seen. Double-click is the same return, by hand.
The media area is the window, at every magnificationThe picture is clipped by the media area's rounded inset and never runs to the pane's edge: the 10px corner and the tile-inset of panel around it belong to the area, not to the picture, so they are there whether the picture is twelve times its fit or a tenth of it. Overflowing in one direction and not the other changes nothing — a panorama still stands inside the same rectangle, with panel showing above and below it. The zoom is a state of the picture, not a mode of the pane: the header keeps its figures, the bar keeps its buttons, the strip keeps its frames, and nothing moves while you zoom.
