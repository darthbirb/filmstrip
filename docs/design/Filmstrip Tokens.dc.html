The scale
Every colour, size, radius and duration the interface may use, and nothing it does not. This is the :root and @theme layer of src/styles/app.css, re-derived from ggallery's drawing rather than lifted from it. The ramp is unchanged where it was already right — it was measured for contrast and it holds. Three things change, and the three cards below say what each buys. The finished CSS is at the foot of this sheet.
Change 01 · type
IBM Plex Sans, bundled

Segoe UI Variable is on every Windows 11 machine but it is not ours to have a voice in, and the app cannot fetch it on any other. Plex Sans ships inside the app under the OFL, has true tabular figures for the counts and sizes this interface is mostly made of, and reads as a tool rather than as a document. Archivo ExtraBold keeps the wordmark, as it already does. Two families, both open, both bundled.

Change 02 · glyphs
Phosphor, regular and fill
Material Symbols is 5.4 MB whole and its hairline weight thins to nothing at 100%. Phosphor is MIT, ships as two small families, and draws the rule the interface already keeps: filled names a thing — a folder, a source, the Sorting Box — and regular names an action. One family per meaning instead of one variable axis nobody can see.

Change 03 · colour
No accent, and focus goes red
One white ring used to carry two meanings: where the keyboard is, and which tile the pane is showing. They are split by giving each its own mark rather than by adding a hue. In the pane is the white ring drawn on the tile, with a white plate in the corner; keyboard focus is a red ring offset clear of the tile, the same red as close. The chrome has no accent at all: the pictures are the colour, and everything else is warm neutral.
A folder's selection stays the pewter plate, and a tile's selection is a checkbox rather than a ring. Red means one thing and it is never decorative — close, and where the keyboard is.

What this sheet does not have
No token for a feature that does not exist. There is no favourite yellow, no warning orange, no success green, no second shadow, no elevation scale — the app has nothing that would draw them. When triage lands, or the storage screen, or duplicate review, each brings its own names in the change that builds it. A token with nothing using it is a value nobody has had to defend.
The plate's glyphs are one size, and it is already in the scale
The video plate was drawn with its steps and its mute at 0.8125 in the pane and 0.875 in the wide rows beside it — the same two controls at two sizes, neither of them named here. No size is added: every glyph in the plate is the scale's glyph, 0.9375rem, play included, because play is already told apart by being the one drawn filled and in white. Sixty-seven glyphs across the pane and artboard sheets moved onto it \u2014 the plate's, the menus' 1rem and the gear's 1.0625rem, all three sizes nobody had named; a fifth entry in the scale would have been a size invented to keep drawings that disagreed. The component sheet’s last fifteen followed: the tile checkbox’s tick at 13 to glyph-small, and a menu row’s tick at 14 with the dropdowns’ carets and row glyphs at 16 to glyph — a tick inside a 20px plate reads at 12, and anything sitting in a row beside words reads at 15. Six more went with them, found in the render rather than the source: the film-strip’s frame steps, whose carets inherited the document’s 16 from a button that declared no size, and the length plate’s play at 10 — both now the plate’s own 15, which is what this card already claimed of them.
How it is held true
Contrast is painted and measured by tokens.test.ts, not asserted here: every ink weight against well, ground, panel and raised, every plate against its own ink. The ratios in the colour table are that test's output. Tailwind's own palette, radii, text sizes and shadows stay cleared, so a familiar class name cannot smuggle a value in.

Motion, specified
Entry 06, drawn frame by frame. The table above carries the tokens it needs: size and swap. The two-digit badge on each block is its entry number in docs/design/BACKLOG.md; where an entry pointed at ggallery's drawing, its structure and behaviour are kept and its values replaced.
06Motion, specifiedtwo durations, one curve, and one rule about which is which
Three things move in this app and they all move for the same reason: a surface is changing size, and the content inside it is changing with it. The size change is 180ms and the content cross-fade is 220ms, the longer one so the fade finishes after the box has settled rather than under it. Nothing else animates: a colour, a chevron and a frame's opacity are 120ms, and everything else is instant.

The fade is longer than the box on purpose180 and 220 are not two guesses at one number. The box settling first is what makes the move read as one panel changing width: if the fade finished first you would see the rail sitting in a panel still moving, which is the two-panels-swapping effect the fold is trying to avoid. Both start together and the box arrives 40ms early.
What does not moveClicking another picture replaces the pane's contents with no transition — a cross-fade between two photographs is a dissolve, and a viewer that dissolves is lying about which file you are looking at. The grid never animates its layout, the strip never scrolls smoothly under a step, and the splitter follows the pointer exactly. Nothing animates on first paint.
A panel opened over the grid is the same moveA folded panel's button lays it over the grid: the same 180ms width, from the rail's 2.75rem to its own, with the one shadow in the app arriving with it. It is not a slide-in from off-screen — the panel was always there, at rail width.
