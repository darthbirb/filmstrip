The whole window, five times
The same interface at four sizes — wide with all three columns docked, narrow with the pane folded, the 640 × 480 minimum, everything at 200% — and then Settings over it. Nothing is laid out against a written-down breakpoint — each artboard shows what the frame does when it measures its own width against the columns' widths and the grid's 20rem minimum, and the pane folds first, navigation second, the grid never. Every size in these drawings is calc(var(--u) × n), where --u stands for 1rem: A, B, C and E set it to 16px, D sets it to 32px, and nothing else about D differs.
The library is src/dev/mock.ts — its sources, its folders, its filenames, its four shapes and its own gradient recipe for a thumbnail. Cairo's three items are all there are, so the grids here carry more of the same shapes to fill the window; nothing else is invented. Every decision from docs/design/BACKLOG.md is in these five windows as well as in the blocks below: the count pills, the folder disclosure and its counts, the picture on the panel, the action bar, the full-screen control, the indexing line above a footer that never moves, the failures banner, and the folded rail's own places.

The interface

Grid layoutRows
A section with nothing in it yet is not in the rail. Each arrives with the slice that builds it.
Settings is a dialog over the window rather than a fourth column or a screen of its own. The frame is three columns and stays three columns; a preference set once a month should not cost the grid a permanent surface, and a dialog can be dismissed with Escape and leaves nothing behind.
01A rail, because this is going to get big. Tabs across the top hold six or seven names and then start scrolling; a rail down the side takes twenty, lets a section be called Tags and aliases rather than Tags, and groups them under headings. The filter above it is how a person finds a row when there are eighty of them. Two rows are drawn because two are built; the shape is sized for the rest.
02The layout toggle now lives here, as a dropdown. It was the widest thing in the grid’s header — a header that has to hold the place’s name first — and it is set once and then left alone. Two glyphs were right in a toolbar, where the pictures beside them said what the choice did; in a list of preferences the choice has to name itself, so it reads Rows.
03Anything with a control on screen stays there. The tile size keeps its two steps in the grid’s header; whether the pane’s details are open is set by opening them, and it is remembered. A state you can set in place does not belong in a dialog — a second control for the same thing is a second thing to keep in sync, and it teaches people to go looking in the wrong place.
04There is no Save, and no help text. Every control writes as you touch it, 400ms after the last change, the way the panel widths already do — a dialog that can be cancelled implies a draft, and there is no draft. Each label is the shortest true name for the thing it sets; a settings row that needs a sentence is a row whose label is wrong.
The gear is in the window bar Left of the caption buttons, at caption-button width, so it belongs to the window rather than to any one column — the same reason search sits there. It is the only thing in the bar that is not Windows’ own furniture or the search field.
Left room, not drawn: sources, tags and aliases, triage hotkeys, trash limits. Each is a group in this dialog when the slice that builds it lands — which is why the groups are stacked and captioned rather than laid out as a fixed set.

The fold button goes because in full screen there is nothing to fold away from, and the full-screen control takes its own place on the right rather than moving into the gap. The row's height, its disclosure and its figures are unchanged.
It works from any state, and puts the pane back in the one it was inDocked, the header's control enters and leaves. Folded or hidden, there is no header row to hold a control, so the way in is a double-click on a tile or the shortcut — a single click still docks the pane, as it already does. Leaving returns the pane to exactly what it was: docked stays docked, folded folds back to its rail. Full screen is a state of the pane, not a place you navigate to.
The bar stays, because the window is still a windowThis is not the display's full screen. Native decorations are off, so the 2.25rem caption strip is the only way to close, minimise or maximise the app — taking it away would leave a window with no furniture. Search and the gear stay with it, and they still belong to the window rather than to the pane.
The filmstrip earns its place here more than anywhereAt 1440 it holds eleven frames instead of three, and it is still the place the picture was clicked in. That is the reason full screen keeps it: with the grid gone, the strip is the only thing on screen that says where you are and what is either side of you.

A folder counts its own items, and says so in words3 here · 0 below rather than one ambiguous number, because a person standing in Trips wants to know whether its two loose files are all of it. A folder with nothing below it still says 0 below, so the shape of the line never changes; a folder with neither says empty. The Sorting Box says what it is waiting on, and the Trash what it is holding.
The chevron sits before the breadcrumb, not after the titleA disclosure's chevron belongs at the leading edge of the thing it opens, and the thing it opens is the whole band. It takes the same 1.25rem square the tree's rows use, so the grid's header and navigation's rows start on one vertical line down the window. Putting it after the title would make it read as a dropdown on the folder's name, which it is not.
Status is a segmented group, and it has no colourThe database allows wip, complete or nothing, so there are exactly three options and all three fit in one group — which finally gives Segmented.tsx a user. ggallery put an orange and a green dot beside them; here the chosen segment wears the raised-hi plate, as every chosen option in the app does. Three words say more than three dots and cost no hue.
The band is the pane's details, one level upSame caption column, same 1.625rem row, same chips, same three families told apart by shape. Nothing new was invented for it — a folder and an item are both things with labels, tags and a path, and they should not need two vocabularies. The band is capped at half the grid's height for the same reason the pane's details are: the pictures come first.

A row with no items of its own shows no pill at all.

A pill, not plain text, because the row has three columns alreadyA bare number at the end of a row competes with the title it sits beside — and at the tail of a long name the two run together. A plate ends the row, groups itself, and holds up against the selection plate underneath: on pewter it becomes a dark wash with on-plate ink, so the pill is still a pill but the row still reads as one filled surface.
An absent pill is informationA 0 in a pill is a number to read and dismiss on every empty folder in the tree. No pill says the same thing in no ink. It is the same reasoning as the pane's dates, which have no Taken line rather than a line saying unknown.
An offline source keeps its folders and loses its pillA walk never empties a source it could not read, so the index's last count is still true — but the app cannot stand behind it while the drive is away, and a stale figure in a fresh plate reads as current. The word offline takes the pill's place, at the pill's size, in the pill's column.

The pill becomes a badge on the glyphThere is no room for a pill at the end of a row when the row is a 2rem square, so the count rides the glyph's top-right corner with a 2px ring in the panel's colour to keep it off the glyph. It is the same number in the same type at the same weight; only the anchor moved. On the selected button it inverts to fg with on-plate ink, because a dark wash on pewter at 0.875rem would be unreadable.
A third button for the tree itselfWithout it the folders are simply gone until you unfold, which makes folding a trap for anyone who folded to see more pictures. The tree button lays the full panel over the grid at its own width — the overlay from 06 — and it closes on a pick or on Escape, leaving the rail as it was. Left open · whether favourite folders belong here tooThe schema has a favourite flag on folders and the rail is where pinned places would naturally live. It is not drawn, because nothing else in the app surfaces that flag yet and a rail is the wrong place to introduce it.
11A scrubber at the grid's edgea 1rem channel, a square thumb, held to jump anywhere
Forty thousand pictures on a thin scrollbar is a thread you cannot aim with. The channel is wide enough to grab without looking, the thumb is a square rather than a lozenge, and while it is held the date under the pointer shows beside it. It replaces the scrollbar rather than sitting beside it — two tracks at one edge is two answers to one question.

The position is the informationThe thumb's length says how much of the folder is on screen and its position says where you are, which in forty thousand files is the only orientation available. Held, the plate adds the date and the item's number — the two things you would be looking for the folder's middle to find out.
It replaces the scrollbar, and appears only when it is neededTwo tracks at one edge is two answers to one question, and the native bar cannot be styled to match anything here. The channel is empty until there is more than a screenful, shows only the thumb at rest, and fills its 1rem with a panel surface under the pointer — the same 120ms as every other hover in the app. The thumb never goes below 1.75rem, however long the folder.
Left open · whether it earns its place at allThis is the one entry the backlog says to consider rather than do, and drawing it has not settled it. It is furniture at the edge of every grid, most of which hold a few hundred files and scroll fine, for a problem that belongs to the largest folders. Replacing a scrollbar also means owning keyboard paging, wheel behaviour and touch, none of which is drawn here. Worth building after the other ten, and only if the large folders still feel unnavigable once they exist.
